## 1. The conceptual core

### 1.1 Naming — one collision to settle first

"Data Vault" is already an established data-modelling methodology (Dan Linstedt: hubs, links, satellites). If we call our thing a "data vault" we collide with it in every search result and every conversation with a data engineer. Proposal — three distinct, defensible terms:

- **Yajur Health Vault** — the whole system: the governed on-premise repository in which identity is *structurally* separated from clinical content.
- **Identity Vault** — the crypto core: the physically separate, encrypted store holding the only mapping from real identity to pseudonym.
- **Gold layer = "the Vault state"** — the property that gold-layer data, on its own, cannot re-identify anyone.

The claim of the paper becomes precise: *the Vault is not a place where secrets are kept; it is an architecture in which the secret is not present.*

### 1.2 The five zones (extends your brief's archive → bronze → silver → gold)

| Zone | Name | PHI present? | Who can read | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Landing / Archive** | Full, encrypted | Nobody, by default | Byte-exact immutable record of what arrived. WORM, retention-locked, content-hashed. Legal record of receipt. Never queried analytically. |
| 1 | **Bronze** | Direct identifiers **extracted out** | Ingestion service only | Parsed and typed. **This is where the split happens** — direct identifiers leave for the Identity Vault; the record keeps only a random `subject_id`. |
| 2 | **Silver** | Quasi-identifiers being reduced | Data engineering, audited | Conformed, deduped, date-shifted; free text passes the de-identification engine; quasi-identifiers generalised. |
| 3 | **Gold** | None | Everyone, freely | Analysis-ready marts, disclosure-controlled. k-anonymity floor enforced as a **gate**, not a hope. The only layer BI tools and models ever touch. |
| 4 | **Egress** | None, verified | Requires sign-off | Statistical disclosure control on anything leaving the boundary at all. |

Invariant to state explicitly and enforce: **identifiability decreases monotonically down the zones, and no zone can reach upward.** Gold has no credential, no network route, and no key that reaches silver or the Identity Vault.

### 1.3 Identifier scheme — a three-layer key hierarchy

**Confirmed by MIMIC:** identifiers are generated by a *random cipher / look-up table*, not by hashing the MRN. MIMIC-IV's own wording: "Patient identifiers were replaced using a random cipher, resulting in deidentified integer identifiers." So there is no dictionary-attack surface against the identifier itself. We do the same.

**Upgraded from SAIL** — SAIL runs a genuine three-layer key hierarchy, and each layer defeats a *different* attack. We adopt it directly:

| Layer | SAIL name | Held by | Attack it defeats |
|---|---|---|---|
| Real identifier | NHS number / ABHA + MRN | The hospital | — |
| 1 | **ALF** — consistent encryption (Blowfish) by the trusted third party | TTP | Exposure of the national identifier |
| 2 | **ALF_E** — re-encrypted on receipt by SAIL | The vault operator | *TTP collusion* — neither party alone can reverse it |
| 3 | **ALF_PE** — encrypted again per project | Per-project key | *Cross-project mosaic* — a researcher on two projects cannot join their own two datasets |

Also worth stealing: **RALF** (Residential ALF) — the same treatment applied to *addresses*, so you can study households and neighbourhood effects without ever knowing a location. That maps straight onto the neighbourhood-wellness thesis of the lakehouse post.

Matching before encryption uses SAIL's **MACRAL** approach — deterministic on a valid national ID where present, probabilistic/weighted on five demographics (forename, surname, postcode, DOB, gender) otherwise. Reported match rates: 99.999% (GP), 99.32% (inpatient), 95.16% (social services).

### 1.4 Date shifting — **corrected**

My first draft of this plan said date shifting destroys seasonality, and built a three-mode "policy switch" to work around it. **That premise was wrong**, and the correction produces a simpler and better design.

MIMIC shifts every record for a patient by a **single patient-level offset in whole days**, and *preserves*: exact intra-patient intervals, **time of day, day of week, and approximate seasonality**. What is actually destroyed is (a) absolute calendar year — shifted years land in 2100–2200 — and (b) **cross-patient temporal comparability**: "two patients admitted in 2130 were not necessarily admitted in the same year."

So the real analytic loss is not seasonality. It is the inability to compare *across* patients in real time — which is what kills epidemic-curve and practice-change studies.

**MIMIC-IV's fix, which we adopt:** an `anchor_year_group` — a **three-year band** of real calendar time (e.g. `2008 - 2010`) carried alongside `anchor_year` and `anchor_age`. This gives back coarse real-world calendar position at ±3 years while keeping per-patient day-level shifts. Dengue and respiratory surveillance work under this; a COVID-wave stratification does not.

**89+ rule:** MIMIC-III set DOB to 300 years before first admission (a sentinel researchers must detect and recode); MIMIC-IV top-codes `anchor_age = 91` and drops `dob` entirely. **We use MIMIC-IV's top-coding** — a sentinel can be silently mis-analysed as a real age; a top-coded value cannot.

### 1.5 Free-text de-identification — **the surrogate question is genuinely open**

My first draft asserted that realistic surrogates are settled modern best practice and that MIMIC's brackets are outdated. The evidence runs the *other way*: MIMIC moved **away** from surrogates over time.

| Release | Replacement convention | Property |
|---|---|---|
| MIMIC-III | Typed **and indexed** tags — `[**Known lastname 1234**]`, `[**Hospital1 18**]` | Informative + stable per entity — but the index **leaks equivalence classes** (you learn which mentions are the same person) |
| MIMIC-IV | **Three underscores `___`** for every PHI instance | Zero information, zero linkage. Safest. But destroys *what kind* of thing was removed — a real cost for downstream NLP |
| `deid` package (optional mode) | Realistic surrogates — fictitious names, MRNs, dates | Camouflage: a detection miss is indistinguishable from a surrogate |

There is a further argument against surrogates that is specific to a *hospital* vault rather than a research corpus: **a clinician reading a surrogate-substituted note cannot tell real from fabricated.** In a system that may ever inform care, inventing plausible clinical facts is a patient-safety hazard, not just a privacy choice.

**DECIDED — both, selected by destination.** The replacement mode is a function of *who reads the text*, which makes this a policy, not a preference:

| Destination | Mode | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Zones 2–3, internal clinical vault; local inference | **Typed, unindexed tags** — `<NAME>`, `<HOSPITAL>`, `<MRN>` | A clinician or model knows *a name was removed and that it was a name*. Nothing is fabricated, so nothing can be misread as a clinical fact. No equivalence-class leak. |
| Zone 4, research-corpus export | **Realistic surrogates**, consistent within a patient, different across patients | Camouflage: a detection miss is indistinguishable from a surrogate. No clinician reads this text, so the safety objection does not apply. |
| Never | **Indexed** tags — `[**Known lastname 1234**]` | The index leaks equivalence classes: you learn which mentions are the same person. Documented as an anti-pattern. |

The demo exposes all four modes on a toggle — `[REDACTED]`, typed tags, surrogates, and the indexed anti-pattern — so a visitor can see the trade-off rather than be told it.

Dates inside notes are *not* tagged — they are shifted consistently with the structured data, so the narrative stays coherent.

**Detection**, in both cases: hybrid and entirely local — dictionary look-up, regex, and context heuristics (the `deid`/Neamatullah lineage) plus a locally-run neural NER, with annotations unioned (MIMIC-IV-Note's method). Never a cloud API.

**Real numbers to quote, with their trade-off:** `deid` achieved **recall 0.967, precision 0.749** on 2,434 nursing notes — deliberately biased toward recall, meaning **~15% of detected "PHI" is false positive and clinically useful text is destroyed as collateral.** MIMIC-IV-Note reports 99.9% sensitivity on radiology reports. Publishing these honestly, with the collateral cost stated, is more persuasive than claiming perfection.

### 1.6 Disclosure control — **scoped down, honestly**

My first draft proposed a k-anonymity floor enforced as a build-time gate on everything moving silver → gold. The research forces a narrower and more defensible claim.

k-anonymity, l-diversity and t-closeness all assume you can cleanly partition columns into quasi-identifiers and sensitive attributes. **Real EHR data cannot be partitioned that way** — a longitudinal sequence of 200 lab values and timestamps *is* a quasi-identifier, and high-dimensional sparse records are provably near-impossible to k-anonymise without destroying utility. Decisively: **neither MIMIC nor SAIL claims k-anonymity, l-diversity or differential privacy anywhere.** Both rely on governance, access control and output checking instead.

So the corrected design:

- **k-anonymity gate applies to gold *aggregate and cohort marts*** — where the QI/sensitive partition is real and the gate is meaningful. Enforced at build time; records that cannot reach the threshold are quarantined, not published. l-diversity check alongside, so a k-anonymous group that is 100% one diagnosis is still caught.
- **Full longitudinal records are not claimed to be k-anonymous.** They are protected by access control, project-scoped pseudonyms (ALF_PE), and **output checking at Zone 4** — the SAIL model.
- The standard we test against is the **ICO "motivated intruder" test** — could someone who *wants* to re-identify a person from this output succeed? — not a checklist of removed fields.

### 1.7 Break-glass re-identification

Six conditions, all required:

1. Role holds `vault.reidentify` (RBAC).
2. Second factor — TOTP or WebAuthn.
3. A **stated purpose** bound to a ticket, consent record, or legal basis.
4. **Dual control** — a second authoriser for anything beyond a single subject.
5. **Time-boxed** session with a visible countdown.
6. **Scoped** to enumerated `subject_id`s. There is no "re-identify all".

Produces an append-only, **hash-chained** audit entry the requester cannot delete or alter.

Satisfies your requirement directly: *users without the permission cannot see that a re-identification occurred, nor which patient was involved, nor who holds the access.* The audit log is visible only to the DPO/custodian role.

Key custody: the re-identification key lives in **OpenBao** (open-source HashiCorp Vault fork), sealed, unsealed by Shamir key shares held by different people. The database administrator cannot decrypt the Identity Vault. Neither can we.

### 1.8 "No data leaves" — structural, not contractual

The claim has to be architectural or it is worthless. Five layers:

1. **Network** — default-deny egress on the Vault VLAN. No route to the internet. Explicit allowlist only.
2. **Runtime** — containers with no outbound DNS; an egress proxy that logs and drops.
3. **Data plane** — models are pulled *in* once, verified by digest, then run locally. Never called out.
4. **Credential** — *no API keys for any external model provider exist anywhere in the Vault environment.* You cannot exfiltrate with a key you do not have. This is the line that makes the argument land with a CIO.
5. **Proof** — continuous egress audit, plus canary records that would alert if they ever surfaced externally.

The framing for the paper: this is not "we promise not to send your data anywhere." It is "there is no mechanism by which your data could be sent anywhere." Those are different claims and only one of them is auditable.

### 1.9 Local inference (Phase 2)

Serving: Ollama / vLLM / llama.cpp. Open-weight models (Llama, Qwen, Gemma, MedGemma-class). Local embeddings; pgvector / Qdrant / LanceDB for retrieval.

Two tiers:
- **Gold-tier inference** — default. Reads anonymised gold. Any output is safe by construction.
- **Sealed clinical inference** — reads identified silver *inside* the boundary for point-of-care use, but its outputs pass back through the same redaction gate before display or storage. Higher access tier, fully audited.

### The India argument — the paper's sharpest and most original point

The research turned up something worth building a section around, because most commentary on this gets it wrong:

1. **DPDP Act 2023 has no "sensitive personal data" category at all.** Health data gets *no special statutory tier* — a deliberate departure from the 2011 SPDI Rules and the withdrawn 2019 Bill. Widely repeated claims that DPDP classifies health data as sensitive are simply wrong, and correcting that in print is a real contribution.
2. **DPDP is entirely silent on anonymisation.** The 2019 Bill had s.91; the Act has nothing. Anonymised data falls outside s.2(t) by inference only — **there is no statutory standard, no safe-harbour list, and no regulator-blessed technique.**
3. **ABDM's HDM Policy Cl. 29.5 delegates the technical standard to anonymisation protocols "which may be specified by the NHA in consultation with MeitY" — and no evidence was found that the NHA has ever published them.**

Put together: *India has a policy requiring anonymisation to follow protocols that do not exist, and an Act that does not define anonymisation.* The practical conclusion for a hospital — and the paper's recommendation — is to **explicitly adopt an external standard (HIPAA Expert Determination and/or the ICO motivated-intruder test) and say so in writing**, because conformance to a domestic standard is not currently available to claim.

Two further accuracy points to carry: the **DPDP Rules 2025 were notified in November 2025** with phased commencement (so the earlier "still being notified" framing is out of date), and the s.17(2)(b) research exemption is **conditional, not blanket** — Rule 16 and the Second Schedule require, among other things, that the processing *not be used to take decisions about specific individuals*.

**Citation hygiene:** `tasks/health-vault-research.md` flags every item the researcher could not verify — the ABDM clause numbers come from the **April 2022 Draft v02** and it is unconfirmed which text is now operative; the exact DPDP Rules gazette date varies across sources; the MIMIC-III tag grammar is not in an official spec. Anything flagged gets verified against the primary source before it goes in the paper, or gets hedged in the prose.

**Internal links** (SEO + the pattern already established): → the lakehouse post (its direct sequel), → the clinical reasoning pipelines post, → the task framework post, → the ethical interoperability post, → the ABDM cancer-care post.

### The "honest limits" section — what goes in it

This section is what makes the paper credible rather than marketing, and I'd push to keep every line of it:

- **Anonymisation is never absolute.** Genomic data, rare diseases, and long longitudinal trajectories are inherently re-identifiable regardless of technique.
- **No formal privacy guarantee is being claimed over the record set.** k-anonymity holds for aggregate marts only. Neither MIMIC nor SAIL claims one either — both fall back on governance, and so do we. Saying this out loud is the difference between a concept paper and a brochure.
- **De-identification destroys real clinical signal.** `deid`'s 0.967 recall came with 0.749 precision — roughly 15% of what it removes is not PHI at all. That is the price, and it should be printed.
- **Cross-patient temporal comparability is gone**, softened but not restored by the ±3-year `anchor_year_group` band. Epidemic-curve work is degraded; wave-level stratification is not possible.
- **The Identity Vault is a *concentrated* risk.** We have not removed the crown jewel; we have made it small, single-purpose and heavily guarded. Better, not perfect. MIMIC is in the same position — it is a *pseudonymised-at-source, key-withheld* release, not a keyless anonymisation. Honesty here is cheap and buys credibility.
- **The ABDM objection, answered head-on.** ABDM is deliberately *federated*: records stay with the originating facility, and the HIE-CM brokers consent-based access so that no national clinical lake is ever created. A hospital vault **is** a local lake, and therefore takes on precisely the risk ABDM's architecture was designed to avoid. This is the strongest argument against the entire Health Vault concept and the paper is not credible unless it states it plainly and answers it — the answer being that the data is already in the hospital, the vault changes *how it is held* rather than *whether it is held*, and the alternative to a governed lake is the ungoverned one that already exists in every hospital's reporting database.

## 3. Deliverable B — the interactive demo

**File:** `sandbox/health-vault-anonymiser.html`
Single self-contained file. Jekyll front matter `sitemap: false`, `{% raw %}` wrapper, vanilla JS, the same dark palette and Plus Jakarta Sans / Space Mono typography as `datalakehouse-navigator.html`. No build step, no server, no dependencies.

**Real cryptography throughout** — WebCrypto `AES-GCM` for the Identity Vault, `HMAC-SHA-1` for genuine TOTP, `SHA-256` for the audit hash chain. Not a mockup of crypto; actual crypto on synthetic data. A "show me the code" panel exposes the exact functions.

### Stages

**0 — Source.** A realistic Indian discharge summary + structured record: name, ABHA number, MRN, phone, address with PIN, admission dates, a free-text narrative naming the patient, a relative, a referring doctor and a hospital. **Editable** — visitors can paste their own text and watch it get anonymised.

**1 — Landing / Archive.** The byte-exact blob, its SHA-256, a WORM lock badge. *"This is what arrived. It is never touched again, and nobody reads it."*

**2 — Bronze / The Split.** Animated: direct identifiers physically fly out of the record into a locked Identity Vault panel, shown as real AES-GCM ciphertext. The clinical record is left holding `subject_id: 10023847`. Two schemas rendered side by side with a visible barrier and the label *no join path exists*.

**3 — Silver / Anonymisation.** Three sub-panels:
- **Date shift** — a slider showing the offset, with a timeline above it proving intervals are preserved while absolute dates move.
- **Free text** — side-by-side diff, every substitution colour-coded by PHI category and hoverable. **A toggle between `[REDACTED]` mode and surrogate mode** so visitors can see for themselves why surrogates win.
- **Generalisation** — PIN 600040 → Chennai district; age 58 → 55–59; rare ICD → parent code.

**4 — Gold / The Vault.** The final record, plus a **k-anonymity meter** showing the cohort this record hides inside (k=1 red → k=12 green). Then the best part: a **"try to re-identify me" challenge** — the visitor gets the gold record and a mock external voter-roll dataset and is invited to attempt a linkage attack. It fails, and the UI shows exactly why.

**5 — Break-glass.** Attempt as *Analyst* → denied, with the reason shown. Switch to *Data Custodian* → state a purpose → enter a live TOTP (genuinely HMAC-generated, with a rotating 30s code) → dual-control second approver → real AES-GCM decrypt streams the true values back into the record under a session countdown. The audit panel appends a hash-chained entry; a **"tamper with this entry"** button visibly breaks the chain. A split-pane shows *what the Analyst sees* — nothing. No trace, no patient, no knowledge that anyone has access.

**6 — Egress guard.** A "Send to external LLM" button that is visibly blocked by the egress policy, packet shown dropped, with the reason *"no credential exists for this destination."* Then a "Run local inference" button that works, showing the model executing inside the boundary.

**Discoverability:** the sticky bottom bar + the inline "Interactive Companion" card, exactly as the lakehouse post does it.
